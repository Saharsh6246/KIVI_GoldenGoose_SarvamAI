# Product Vision: The Things Kivi Comes to Know

## What semantic memory should let Kivi become

Kivi sees something no other product sees, which is the outbound stream of a
working day. Not documents, not meetings, not chats with an assistant. The
things a person actually said, to real people, in the app where it mattered,
forty times a day. Styles and phonetic memory already make that stream
well-written and correctly spelled. Semantic memory should make it recoverable.

Kivi should become the memory of your working voice. Today it's a typist that
forgets. It should be the only system that can answer "what did I tell Priya
about the migration?" or "find what I dictated at 5 PM yesterday in Slack and
make it meeting-ready", because it was the only thing present for all of it.

## Where the real value is created

Not in personalisation. Dictation is already personal, and Styles solved that.
The value is recall of your own record. Nobody remembers what they told whom, in
which thread, on which day, and the cost of that is rewriting what you already
wrote and losing the good sentence you said once in a hurry. Kivi is the one
tool that heard all of it. Retrieval, reuse and honest answers over that history
are worth more than any inference we could draw from it.

## What deserves to be remembered

There are three layers, in order of confidence.

Episodes are the spine. Every dictation, with its raw and formatted text, time,
application and destination. They require no inference at all, and on their own
they answer most of the useful questions.

Facts are entities that survive repetition: people, projects, repos, acronyms. A
thing becomes a fact because you said it many times across many episodes, never
because a model found it interesting once.

Preferences are learned from behaviour, not personality. The corrections you
make, the rewrites you ask for. "Shorter in Slack" is earned by you shortening
Slack messages three times.

The discipline binding all three is that a memory must be traceable to an
utterance. Kivi believes nothing it cannot point at.

## What Kivi must never assume

That mentioning something means being it. Dictating "tell them I'm exhausted" is
a message you sent, not a fact about your health. That you are the subject of
your own dictations, when most of what you say is about other people. That
absence is evidence: if the history has no answer, the answer is "I don't have
that", never a plausible guess. That one correction is a standing rule. That
confidence is correctness.

This rules out things we could impressively build. No screen capture or
always-on listening to enrich memory, because Kivi remembers only what you
deliberately spoke into it. No psychographic or sentiment profile. No proactive
interruptions based on what Kivi thinks you need. And no memory that silently
alters ordinary dictation, because dictation is a motor act with no time to
explain itself, and an unseen intervention is indistinguishable from a
transcription error. Memory speaks only when spoken to. It never changes what
your voice becomes. It changes what Kivi can do when you ask it something.

## Why someone should trust it enough to keep using it

Three promises, enforced in the architecture rather than in the copy.

Nothing is remembered that you did not say. Every answer cites the dictations
behind it, or admits it has none. And forgetting is real, because memories are
derived views over your dictations, so deleting a dictation deletes everything
it taught. That is not the industry norm, where erasing a conversation leaves
its conclusions behind.

A person should be able to use Kivi for a year without ever administering it,
and still be able to ask, at any moment, why it knows that.
