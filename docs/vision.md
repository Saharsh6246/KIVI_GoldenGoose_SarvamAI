# Product Vision: What Kivi Comes to Know
 
## What semantic memory should let Kivi become
 
Kivi sees something no other product sees: the outbound stream of a working day.
Not documents, not meetings, not chats with an assistant. The things a person
actually said, to real people, in the app where it mattered, forty times a day.
Styles and phonetic memory already make that stream well-written and correctly
spelled. Semantic memory should make it recoverable.
 
Kivi should become the memory of your working voice. Today it's a typist that
forgets. It should be the only system that can answer "what did I tell Priya
about the migration?" or "find what I dictated at 5 PM yesterday in Slack and
make it meeting-ready", because it was present for all of it.
 
## Where the real value is created
 
Not in personalisation. Dictation is already personal, and Styles solved that.
The value is recall of your own record. Nobody remembers what they told whom, in
which thread, on which day, and the cost is rewriting what you already wrote and
losing the good sentence you said once in a hurry. Kivi heard all of it. Retrieval, reuse and honest answers over that history are worth
more than any inference drawn from it.
 
## What deserves to be remembered
 
Three layers, in order of confidence.
 
Episodes are the spine. Every dictation, with its raw and formatted text, time,
application and destination. They require no inference, and on their own answer
most useful questions.
 
Facts are entities that survive repetition: people, projects, repos, acronyms.
Something becomes a fact because you said it many times across episodes,
never because a model found it interesting once.
 
Preferences are learned from behaviour, not personality. The corrections you
make, the rewrites you ask for. "Shorter in Slack" is earned by shortening Slack
messages three times.
 
The discipline binding all three: a memory must be traceable to an utterance.
Kivi believes nothing it cannot point at.
 
## What Kivi must never assume
 
That mentioning something means being it. Dictating "tell them I'm exhausted" is
a message you sent, not a fact about your health. That you are the subject of
your own dictations; most of what you say is about other people. That absence is
evidence: if the history has no answer, the answer is "I don't have that", never
a plausible guess. That one correction is a standing rule. That confidence is
correctness.
 
This rules out things we could impressively build. No screen capture or
always-on listening to enrich memory: Kivi remembers only what you deliberately
spoke into it. No psychographic or sentiment profile. No proactive interruptions
based on what Kivi thinks you need. And no memory that silently alters ordinary
dictation. Dictation is a motor act with no time to explain itself, and an
unseen intervention is indistinguishable from a transcription error. Memory
speaks only when spoken to. It never changes what your voice becomes. It changes
what Kivi can do when you ask it something.
 
## Why someone should trust it enough to keep using it
 
Three promises, enforced in the architecture, not the copy.
 
Nothing is remembered that you did not say. Every answer cites the dictations
behind it, or admits it has none. And forgetting is real: memories are derived
views over your dictations, so deleting a dictation deletes everything it
taught. Not the industry norm, where erasing a conversation leaves its
conclusions behind.
 
A person should be able to use Kivi for a year without ever administering it,
and still ask, at any moment, why it knows that.
