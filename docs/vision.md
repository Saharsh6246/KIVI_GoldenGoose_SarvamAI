# Product Vision — The Things Kivi Comes to Know

## What semantic memory should let Kivi become

Kivi sees something no other product sees: the outbound stream of a working day. Not documents, not meetings, not chats with an assistant — the things a person actually said, to real people, in the app where it mattered, forty times a day. Styles and phonetic memory make that stream well-written and correctly spelled. Semantic memory should make it **recoverable**.

Kivi should become the memory of your working voice. Today it is a typist that forgets. It should become the only system that can answer *"what did I tell Priya about the migration?"* or *"find what I dictated at 5 PM yesterday in Slack and make it meeting-ready"* — because it was the only thing present for all of it.

## Where the real value is created

Not in personalisation. Dictation is already personal; Styles solved that. The value is **recall of your own record**. Nobody remembers what they told whom, in which thread, on which day — and the cost is rewriting what you already wrote and losing the good sentence you said once in a hurry. Kivi is the one tool that heard all of it. Retrieval, reuse, and honest answers over that history are worth more than any inference drawn from it.

## What deserves to be remembered

Three layers, in order of confidence.

**Episodes** are the spine: every dictation, with its raw and formatted text, time, application, and destination. They require no inference, and alone answer most useful questions.

**Facts** are entities that survive repetition: people, projects, repos, acronyms. A thing becomes a fact because you said it many times across many episodes, never because a model found it interesting once.

**Preferences** are learned from behaviour, not personality: the corrections you make, the rewrites you ask for. "Shorter in Slack" is earned by you shortening Slack messages three times.

The discipline binding all three: **a memory must be traceable to an utterance.** Kivi believes nothing it cannot point at.

## What Kivi must never assume

That mentioning something means being it — dictating "tell them I'm exhausted" is a message you sent, not a fact about your health. That you are the subject of your own dictations; most of what you say is about other people. That absence is evidence — if the history has no answer, the answer is "I don't have that," never a plausible guess. That one correction is a standing rule. That confidence is correctness.

**This rules out things we could impressively build.** No screen capture or always-on listening to enrich memory — Kivi remembers only what you deliberately spoke into it. No psychographic or sentiment profile. No proactive interruptions based on what Kivi thinks you need. No memory that silently alters ordinary dictation: dictation is a motor act with no time to explain itself, and an unseen intervention is indistinguishable from a transcription error. **Memory speaks only when spoken to.** It never changes what your voice becomes; it changes what Kivi can do when you ask it something.

## Why someone should trust it enough to keep using it

Three promises, enforced in the architecture, not the copy.

Nothing is remembered that you did not say. Every answer cites the dictations behind it, or admits it has none. And forgetting is real: memories are derived views over your dictations, so deleting a dictation deletes everything it taught — not the industry norm, where erasing a conversation leaves its conclusions behind.

A person should be able to use Kivi for a year without ever administering it, and still be able to ask, at any moment, why it knows that.
